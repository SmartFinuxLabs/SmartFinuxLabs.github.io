# TOKEN2049 Singapore — Day 1: From Tokenization to the Infrastructure Underneath It

Singapore, October 5, 2026

![Singapore Conference & Skyline Recap](./Singapore%20Conference%20%26%20Skyline%20Recap.png)
*Singapore Conference & Skyline Recap — Taisu Ventures RWA Capital Forum, Grego AI Risky Business at CALI by Raffles Place, and Singapore Downtown Skyline.*

My first full day around TOKEN2049 Singapore started with a useful reminder: the digital-asset conversation is moving beyond the token itself.

Across the events I attended and, perhaps more importantly, the people I met, the recurring questions were about infrastructure: how institutional capital enters onchain markets, how risk propagates through interconnected protocols, how tokenized assets obtain liquidity, and how the new rails connect back to custody, settlement, banking and the existing financial system.



## RWA Capital Forum — capital meets onchain infrastructure

The first major stop was the **RWA Capital Forum**, hosted by [Taisu Ventures](https://www.taisuventures.com/?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

The forum brought together institutional investors, digital-asset funds, family offices, liquidity providers and RWA companies around a straightforward question: **where are the most compelling opportunities emerging in onchain real-world assets?** Its scope covered private credit, tokenized assets, institutional yield and next-generation financial infrastructure. [Luma](https://luma.com/ydaq5h18?tk=hogEVV\&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

One of the Taisu presentations framed the market particularly well. Its investment landscape spans four connected areas: infrastructure and tooling; DeFi, including stablecoins, payment rails and RWA tokenization; AI × blockchain; and consumer platforms.

For me, the important point was not simply that RWAs are attracting capital. Tokenization creates an entire stack of requirements underneath the asset: issuance, liquidity, custody, settlement, compliance, accounting, interoperability and eventually integration with conventional financial infrastructure.

That was very close to the questions we have been exploring at Connextium: what happens after an asset or payment becomes digital, and how do we build reliable financial infrastructure around the resulting transaction?

The afternoon therefore felt less like a discussion about a new asset class and more like a discussion about the gradual reconstruction of financial-market infrastructure.

## Risky Business — every dependency becomes part of the risk model

From the RWA forum I moved to **Risky Business – Singapore '26**, organized by [Grego AI](https://www.grego.ai/?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

This was a very different format and a useful counterpoint to the investment discussion earlier in the day.

Rather than a conventional conference panel, Grego designed the event as a curated gathering of DeFi protocol teams, vault providers, strategy builders, risk curators, investors and allocators. The central proposition was simple but important: protocols increasingly depend upon other protocols, lending markets, bridges and oracles, and **every additional dependency creates another surface that can fail**. [Luma](https://luma.com/dupkf78j?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

That idea stayed with me.

Distributed financial infrastructure does not eliminate dependencies. It redistributes them.

As systems become increasingly composable, understanding those dependencies becomes part of understanding the financial product itself. Smart-contract security is one layer, but architecture, external integrations, liquidity dependencies and operational controls form part of the same risk topology.

Grego's approach is particularly interesting in this context: its security work combines human-led engagements with AI-scaled vulnerability analysis. [Luma](https://luma.com/dupkf78j?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

For an enterprise-oriented DLT architecture, this leads to a broader design principle: **composability without dependency visibility is not sufficient infrastructure.**

## Skyline Social — moving from sessions to relationships

The evening shifted toward networking with **Skyline Social Singapore**, another Taisu Ventures gathering in Downtown Singapore. Luma categorizes the event around social and networking rather than formal conference programming. [Luma](https://luma.com/token2049sg2026?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

That transition was valuable. After a day of discussions around RWA capital and protocol risk, conversations became less structured and more exploratory: who is building what, which markets they are working in, and where two different pieces of infrastructure might eventually connect.

And this is where Day 1 became particularly useful.

## Day 1 takeaway: the token is only one layer

The strongest theme I took from Day 1 was not simply “RWA,” “stablecoins,” “AI” or “DeFi.”

It was **infrastructure**.

The RWA Capital Forum looked at where capital can move onchain. Risky Business examined what happens when increasingly interconnected infrastructure introduces new dependencies. Conversations with people working across tokenization, treasury, partnerships, security and capital-market infrastructure then filled in the space between those two questions.

The next phase of digital finance will require more than issuing assets onchain.

It will require the less visible machinery underneath them: **risk controls, liquidity, custody, accounting, clearing, settlement, compliance, interoperability and connectivity back into the banking system.**

That is the part of TOKEN2049 Singapore I am most interested in exploring over the coming days.

**Day 1: capital → tokenization → infrastructure → risk → relationships.**

A strong start to the week.
