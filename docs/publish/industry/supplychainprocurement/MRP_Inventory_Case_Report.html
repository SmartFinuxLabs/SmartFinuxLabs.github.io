01 · Case definition

Demand is known; supply must arrive on time

A company purchases Part 982 and uses material requirements planning (MRP) to determine when and how much to order. It begins with 5 units on hand. Two purchase orders are already open: 6 units due in week 1 and 10 units due in week 3. Gross requirements are 10, 8, 2, 1, and 12 units across the five weeks.

Requirements are assumed due by the end of each week. For these calculations, safety stock is zero, receipts are usable within their assigned week, and unmet demand carries forward. The original static grid retains the week 3 receipt even though MRP proposes expediting it.

03 · Calculation method

Inventory is a running balance

PABₜ = PABₜ₋₁ + SRₜ + PORcₜ − GRₜ

PAB is projected available balance; SR is scheduled receipts; PORc is planned order receipts; GR is gross requirements. The subscript t identifies the current week. Planned order releases (PORl) authorize future supply and are not added to inventory in the release week.

06 · Weekly walkthrough

How each weekly balance is calculated

01

Stock and an open order cover demand

The company begins with 5 units and receives 6 units from an open purchase order. Demand of 10 units leaves 1 unit available.

5 + 6 − 10 = 1
02

A timing shortage emerges

Only 1 unit is available against demand of 8. The −7 balance represents unmet demand, not physical negative stock. MRP recommends moving the existing 10-unit receipt from week 3 to week 2. If confirmed, the revised balance becomes 3 units.

Original: 1 − 8 = −7 · Expedited: 1 + 10 − 8 = 3
03

The original receipt clears the backlog

In the unchanged schedule, the 10-unit receipt covers the 7-unit backlog and this week’s 2-unit requirement. The balance recovers to 1, but week 2 demand was fulfilled late. After expediting, no receipt remains in week 3: 3 − 2 also leaves 1.

−7 + 10 − 2 = 1
04

Inventory reaches zero; release the new order

Demand of 1 uses the remaining inventory. Release a new purchase order for 20 units in week 4 so that it arrives in week 5, using the one-week lead time. A release does not itself increase on-hand inventory.

1 − 1 = 0 · Release week = 5 − 1 = 4
05

Lot sizing leaves an ending balance of eight

With no opening balance, demand of 12 creates a net requirement of 12. The fixed order quantity of 20 produces a planned receipt of 20 and leaves 8 units after demand is met.

0 + 20 − 12 = 8
07 · Purchasing action plan

Convert MRP recommendations into supply

Action A · Reschedule an open order

Receive 10 units in week 2

Ask the supplier to bring the existing week 3 purchase order forward. Confirm feasibility before the week 2 requirement is due, then update its scheduled receipt date.

Effect: week 2 PAB improves from −7 to +3. Remove the receipt from week 3 to avoid counting the same order twice.

Action B · Release a new order

Order 20 units in week 4

Convert the planned order into a purchase order, using the 20-unit fixed quantity and one-week lead time. Confirm delivery for week 5.

Effect: the week 5 requirement of 12 is covered and 8 units remain.

An expedite message does not guarantee earlier delivery. Until the supplier confirms and the plan is updated, the week 2 shortage remains in the original schedule. If expediting is infeasible, the planner must investigate an alternative source or revised fulfillment timing.
08 · Reconciliation and conclusion

The horizon balances, but timing still matters

5 opening + 16 scheduled + 20 planned − 33 required = 8 ending units

Total scheduled supply is 6 + 10 = 16 units; total demand is 10 + 8 + 2 + 1 + 12 = 33 units. Expediting preserves these totals and removes the projected week 2 backlog under the weekly timing assumptions. The new fixed-quantity order covers week 5 demand while leaving 8 units because of the lot-size rule.

Planning lesson: an adequate total supply does not ensure timely fulfillment. MRP coordinates receipt dates, quantities, and release dates so that inventory is available in the period when it is required.
